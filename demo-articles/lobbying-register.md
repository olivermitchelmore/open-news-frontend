The idea was simple enough to fit on a single page. A public register, updated monthly, listing every paid lobbyist who met a minister, a senior civil servant or a special adviser, and what they discussed.

It took six years, four ministers and one leaked spreadsheet to get it anywhere near reality.

Documents obtained by Open News under freedom of information laws show that the proposal was passed between at least three government departments between 2020 and 2025, each of which agreed with it in principle and argued that another department was better placed to run it.

## A question of cost

Internal estimates put the cost of a register at under £2m a year. But officials repeatedly raised concerns about who would police it, how disputes would be resolved and whether small organisations would be caught by accident.

"Every time we got close, someone found a reason why it was complicated," said a former official who worked on the proposal. "And it was complicated. But so is everything worth doing."

## The leak that changed things

The turning point came last year, when a spreadsheet of ministerial meetings that had not been published was sent anonymously to several newspapers. It showed dozens of meetings with firms bidding for public contracts that had not appeared in official returns.

Within weeks, a register was included in the government's legislative programme. Whether it will be given the resources and powers to work is, campaigners say, the next fight.
