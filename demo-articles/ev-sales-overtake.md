Battery electric cars outsold petrol models in Spain, Italy and Poland for the first time last month, according to industry registration figures, joining the Nordic countries, the Netherlands and Germany in passing the milestone.

The shift has been driven largely by a new generation of smaller, cheaper cars. Half a dozen models now sell for under €25,000 before subsidies, and several manufacturers have cut prices this year to compete with imports.

"For years the question was whether people in southern and eastern Europe would buy electric cars at all," said Marta Novak, an analyst in Warsaw. "It turns out they just wanted ones they could afford."

## Charging catches up

The number of public fast chargers in the three countries has more than doubled in two years, and motorway service stations now routinely offer rapid charging. Surveys suggest range anxiety has fallen sharply as a reason for not buying an electric car.

Petrol and diesel still account for the majority of cars on the road, and hybrids remain popular. But manufacturers expect battery electric models to make up more than half of all new car sales across the EU by 2028.
