A consortium of universities and research institutes has released an artificial intelligence model that performs on a par with the most capable commercial systems, and made its weights, training code and data documentation freely available.

On a widely used suite of reasoning, coding and language benchmarks, the model scored within two points of the best proprietary systems, and ahead of them on several tasks involving scientific literature. It was trained for roughly a tenth of the reported cost of its closed rivals.

"The assumption has been that only three or four companies in the world could build something like this," said Dr Ingrid Solberg, one of the project's leads. "We wanted to test that assumption. It turns out to be wrong."

## Why openness matters

Because the model's weights are public, researchers can inspect how it works, test it for bias and adapt it for specialist uses without sending sensitive data to a third party. Hospitals and public bodies have already expressed interest in running it on their own servers.

Critics argue that releasing powerful models openly makes them easier to misuse. The consortium says it carried out extensive safety testing before release and has published the results, along with a list of known limitations.

## Running it yourself

The full model requires a cluster of high-end graphics cards, but smaller versions can run on a single workstation. Within hours of the release, developers had published versions compressed to run on a laptop.
