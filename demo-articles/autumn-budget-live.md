The Chancellor will set out the government's tax and spending plans to the House of Commons at 12.30, in a statement that ministers have spent weeks insisting will be "responsible, not radical".

Borrowing costs have edged higher since the summer, and the Treasury is under pressure to show that its plans add up without the kind of emergency measures that rattled markets in previous years.

## What we expect

- A freeze on fuel duty for another year, at a cost of around £3bn
- New funding for hospital waiting lists, paid for by a levy on large property developments
- Changes to the rules on public investment, allowing more borrowing for long-term projects
- No change to headline rates of income tax, national insurance or VAT

## The view from the backbenches

Several MPs on the government side have said privately that they want to see more spending on local councils, many of which are warning of effective bankruptcy within two years. The Treasury is expected to offer a one-off grant, but not the multi-year settlement councils have asked for.

> The question is not whether the numbers add up this year. It is whether anyone believes they will add up in three years' time.
>
> Senior economist, speaking before the statement

## Market reaction

Government bond yields were steady in early trading. Analysts said investors had largely priced in the expected measures, and the bigger risk was a surprise in the independent forecasts published alongside the statement.
