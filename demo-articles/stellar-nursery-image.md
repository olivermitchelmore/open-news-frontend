A new space telescope has released its first full-colour image, showing a vast cloud of gas and dust in which hundreds of stars are being born.

The image, captured in infrared light, reveals structures that are invisible to optical telescopes because they are hidden behind thick dust. Pillars of gas several light years tall are being sculpted by radiation from young, massive stars at the heart of the cloud.

"This is the kind of picture that makes you remember why you became an astronomer," said Professor Tomás Varga, a member of the mission's science team. "But the real value is in the data behind it. We can now watch star formation happening in places we simply could not see before."

## What comes next

The telescope will spend its first year surveying star-forming regions across the galaxy, building a census of how stars and planetary systems form. Scientists hope the data will help answer one of astronomy's oldest questions: how common are systems like our own?
