At ten o'clock on a weekday morning, the central library is already full. Students are claiming the long tables by the windows. A knitting group has taken over a corner. In the children's section, a dozen toddlers are sitting in a circle, mostly listening to a story about a bear.

Library visits have risen for three years in a row and are now at their highest level in 15 years, reversing a long decline that saw hundreds of branches close.

"People talk about libraries as if they are about books," said the branch manager, who has worked here for 19 years. "Books are a big part of it. But mostly they are about having somewhere to go."

## A third place

Sociologists call it the "third place": somewhere that is neither home nor work, where people can spend time without being expected to buy anything. As the cost of cafés, gyms and other social spaces has risen, libraries have become one of the few such places left.

Many have adapted. This library now lends laptops, runs job clubs and has a recording studio in the basement that is booked solid most evenings.

## The funding question

The revival has come despite, not because of, funding. Budgets remain well below their levels of a decade ago, and many branches rely on volunteers. Librarians worry that the extra demand is stretching staff to breaking point.
