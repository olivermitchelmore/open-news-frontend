Global stock markets posted their strongest day in four months on Thursday after central banks in Europe and the United States signalled that the long cycle of interest rate rises was over.

Major European indices closed up between 1.8 and 2.4 per cent, while government bond yields fell sharply as investors bet that the next move in rates would be down. Housebuilders and retailers, among the sectors most exposed to borrowing costs, led the gains.

"The message was about as clear as central bankers ever get," said Laura Stein, chief strategist at an investment firm in Frankfurt. "They are not declaring victory over inflation, but they are saying the war is mostly won."

## Why now

Inflation has fallen steadily through the year as energy prices eased and supply chains returned to normal. Core inflation, which strips out volatile items such as food and fuel, is now within half a percentage point of target in most large economies.

Policymakers also pointed to signs of a cooling jobs market. Vacancies have fallen for nine consecutive months, and wage growth, while still strong, has begun to slow.

## Risks remain

Not everyone is convinced. Some economists warn that cutting rates too early could allow inflation to return, particularly if oil prices rise again over the winter. Others say the bigger risk is waiting too long and tipping economies into recession.

For mortgage holders, the more immediate effect may be on fixed-rate deals. Several lenders cut their rates within hours of the announcements.
