When streaming services took off, the received wisdom was that cinemas would struggle to survive. Large multiplexes have indeed found the past few years difficult. But small, independent cinemas are having something of a golden age.

Admissions at independent venues are up by a fifth on pre-pandemic levels, and several new cinemas have opened this year in towns that had not had one for decades.

"We are not competing with streaming," said the programmer at a 90-seat cinema in a converted chapel. "We are competing with the pub. People want a night out, and we give them a night out with a film in the middle."

## The formula

The successful venues share a few traits: a carefully curated programme that mixes new releases with classics, regular events such as director talks and themed nights, and a café or bar that keeps people around before and after the screening.

Many have also built strong membership schemes, which provide steady income and a loyal audience willing to take a chance on unfamiliar films.
