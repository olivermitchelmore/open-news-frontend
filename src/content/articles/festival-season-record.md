The summer festival season drew record crowds this year, with more than five million tickets sold across the country's major events, according to figures from the industry's trade body.

But the average price of a weekend ticket has risen by almost 40 per cent in three years, and a survey of festival-goers found that nearly half had cut back on other spending to afford them.

"Everything costs more: the stages, the security, the fuel for the generators, the artists," said the director of one mid-sized festival. "We have held our prices as long as we can."

## Squeezed in the middle

Large festivals have largely weathered the rising costs, and small, volunteer-run events have survived on goodwill. It is mid-sized independent festivals that have struggled most, with at least a dozen cancelling or pausing this year.

Some are experimenting with payment plans, cheaper day tickets and pay-what-you-can allocations for local residents. Others are asking whether the traditional three-day format still makes sense.
