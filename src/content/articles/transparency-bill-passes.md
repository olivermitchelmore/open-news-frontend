A bill that its supporters say will transform how politicians disclose their contacts with lobbyists and donors cleared its final parliamentary stage shortly before dawn on Wednesday, after an overnight sitting in which opponents tabled more than 60 amendments.

The Public Integrity Bill requires ministers and senior officials to publish details of meetings with lobbyists within 30 days, down from the current quarterly returns that often appear months late. It also creates an independent register of political donations, replacing the patchwork of declarations currently held by different bodies.

"For the first time, anyone will be able to see who is talking to the people in charge, and when," said the bill's sponsor as it passed its third reading by 312 votes to 241.

## The sticking points

Opponents argued that the reporting rules would be burdensome for charities and small businesses, which often meet ministers informally. A late amendment exempts organisations with fewer than ten staff unless they are paid to represent a third party.

Transparency campaigners broadly welcomed the bill, though some said the penalties for late disclosure, capped at £5,000, were too low to change behaviour.

The measures are expected to come into force in April, once guidance for government departments has been published.
