Neuroscientists have produced the most detailed picture yet of how the brain decides which experiences to keep, identifying a group of cells in the hippocampus that appear to act like an index, tagging memories for transfer into long-term storage.

The study, published this week, followed the activity of thousands of individual neurons in mice as they explored new environments and then slept. Specific patterns of activity during exploration were replayed during deep sleep, and the memories that were replayed most often were the ones the animals retained days later.

"It is a bit like a librarian working the night shift," said the study's lead author, Dr Hana Kovač. "During the day, everything gets piled on the desk. At night, the librarian decides what goes on the shelves and what goes in the bin."

## Why it matters

The findings could help explain why sleep deprivation damages memory, and may eventually point towards treatments for conditions such as Alzheimer's disease, in which the hippocampus is among the first regions affected.

The researchers caution that the work was carried out in mice and that the human brain is considerably more complex. They are now planning studies using non-invasive imaging in human volunteers.
