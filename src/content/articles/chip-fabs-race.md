Three of the world's largest chipmakers broke ground on new fabrication plants this week, as demand for the processors that power artificial intelligence continues to outstrip supply.

Together, the plants in Arizona, northern Japan and eastern Germany represent more than $60bn of investment. Each will take at least three years to build and will produce chips on the most advanced manufacturing processes available.

## The talent gap

Industry leaders say the constraint on growth is no longer capital or government support, both of which are plentiful, but people. A modern fab needs thousands of engineers and technicians, many of them with highly specialised skills.

"We can pour concrete faster than we can train process engineers," said the chief operating officer of one of the companies. Several firms have launched partnerships with local universities and technical colleges to create apprenticeship routes into the industry.

## Is it a bubble?

Some analysts worry that the rush to build capacity could lead to a glut if demand for AI hardware cools. The chip industry has a long history of boom and bust cycles. For now, however, order books are full into 2028.
