Open up the latest generation of flagship smartphones and you will find something that has been missing for a decade: a battery that can be removed with a standard screwdriver.

The change is a direct result of right-to-repair rules that came into force in Europe this year. Manufacturers must now make batteries user-replaceable, supply spare parts for at least seven years after a model goes off sale, and publish repair manuals.

"Two years ago, the industry said this was impossible without making phones thicker, heavier and less waterproof," said Jonas Weber, who runs an independent repair shop in Berlin. "The new phones are the same thickness and just as waterproof. Funny, that."

## Longer lives

Repair shops report a surge in business, particularly battery replacements, which now cost around a third of what they did. Early data suggests people are keeping their phones for longer, which campaigners say will significantly reduce electronic waste.

Manufacturers, for their part, have started to present repairability as a selling point. Several now advertise repair scores alongside camera and battery specifications.
