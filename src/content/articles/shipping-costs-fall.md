The cost of shipping a container from Asia to northern Europe has fallen to its lowest level since 2020, according to industry benchmarks, as a wave of newly built vessels enters service and a key trade corridor reopens after two years of disruption.

The spot rate for a standard 40-foot container on the route has dropped below $1,500, less than a fifth of its peak. Rates on transpacific routes have followed a similar pattern.

"We ordered a lot of ships when rates were sky-high," said Henrik Aalto, an analyst at a maritime consultancy in Copenhagen. "Those ships are now arriving, and there simply isn't enough cargo to fill them all."

## Good news for shoppers

Lower freight costs typically feed through to consumer prices with a lag of six to nine months. Retailers say the savings will help offset higher wage costs, and some expect prices for furniture, electronics and clothing to fall in the new year.

## Bad news for shipping lines

For the carriers, the outlook is bleaker. Several have announced plans to scrap older vessels or slow their ships to reduce capacity, and at least two smaller lines are reported to be in merger talks.
